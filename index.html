Machine unlearning · trustworthy AI · multimodal systems

Building AI systems that can forget — and proving when they have not.

I develop machine-unlearning methods, audits and research software for neural, multimodal and vision-language systems. The work asks a simple question with difficult consequences: when a model appears to forget, what evidence is strong enough to believe it?

01

Research thesis

Forgetting is not a score. It is a claim about behaviour, privacy, internal representation and what survives deployment.

My research treats machine unlearning as a systems problem. A useful method must remove targeted information, preserve retained capability, withstand deeper audits and remain forgotten after the model is transformed for real deployment.

01

Selective forgetting

Remove targeted information without paying the full cost of retraining or destroying useful capability.

02

Representation audit

Look beneath output accuracy for recoverable or identifiable structure that may still encode the forgotten target.

03

Evaluation reliability

Test disagreement between forgetting, retention, privacy and representation metrics instead of trusting one scalar.

04

Deployment durability

Stress unlearned models after quantisation and other transformations to see whether supposedly forgotten information returns.

About

I work where research claims meet implementation details.

I am a final-stage PhD researcher in Computer Science based in Perth. My research focuses on trustworthy and privacy-preserving AI, especially machine unlearning in neural, multimodal and vision-language systems.

Alongside research, I build reproducible experimentation pipelines, applied AI systems and interactive teaching material. I care about whether a method is not only novel, but measurable, reproducible and useful.
